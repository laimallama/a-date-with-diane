Notes on this edition of A Date with Diane

This edition is a restored and expanded version of an old standalone HTML text game, A Date with Diane. The original author is unknown. The game comes from an older period of personal web games — plain HTML, simple JavaScript, text-only play — with a clearly British setting: pounds, buses, pubs, theatre foyers, and a style of comic adult embarrassment that is part of its charm.

The original already had a strong concept: a branching adult date story built around desperation, embarrassment, intimacy, missed chances, and steering the evening toward different outcomes. Its best quality was never polished literary prose, but a cheeky, practical, slightly old-fashioned voice — attentive to timing, queues, drinks, toilets, and social awkwardness. This edition keeps that voice.

The aim was not to remake the game in a new style, but to make it complete, readable, and properly preserved: fix distracting grammar and formatting, clean the interface into a text-only presentation, add Back with full state restore, expose useful status (including Shyness), repair broken or misleading branches, and finish the usable endings.

The game is available in five independent languages — English, Simplified Chinese, Traditional Chinese (Taiwan-flavoured), Spanish, and French — each as its own HTML file, with bilingual editions (paired with English) for every language except English itself. Translations are localized, not word-for-word: Chinese in particular was edited carefully against machine-translation tone and awkward toilet wording; Spanish and French were checked for natural adult-game phrasing and local punctuation.

Verified ending and hidden-scene routes were found by reading the JavaScript, tracing branches, and replaying them with scripts. Those routes power the in-game Gallery: choose an ending or hidden scene and the game highlights the correct choice at each step. You can turn the guide highlighting off and on, go Back with full state restore, and — on ending routes — Skip to the good bit. There are no separate click-path guide text files; the Gallery is the walkthrough.

Alongside the HTML, each language folder keeps payoff transcripts under transcripts/endings and transcripts/hidden_scenes. These are not full playthroughs and not guides. Each file is the segment of one Gallery entry from where that ending or hidden scene really begins (the same cut as Skip to the good bit / the start of a hidden scene), titled with that entry’s Gallery name, in Gallery order.

A small maintenance/ toolkit remains for route smoke tests, Gallery data rebuilds, transcript regeneration, and the aligned-text index. It is not needed to play; it is useful when editing text, translations, Gallery routes, or transcripts. Start with maintenance/AI_HANDOFF.md.

This edition is best understood as restoration and completion rather than remake: clean presentation, repaired logic, finished endings, translations, Gallery walkthroughs, and payoff transcripts — so the original personality of the game can come through more clearly.
