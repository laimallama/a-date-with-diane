You head towards the front door.

You can hear footsteps in the room above. That will be your brother. So there's nobody in the bathroom.

You're not sure whether Diane realises that. Maybe she does, maybe she doesn't.

Are you going to tell her?

(No, I don't think I'll mention it.)

You are at your front door with Diane, ready to say goodnight. You've had a great evening. She lives quite near and you don't need to see her home.

But –

She's really desperate for the loo. She can't stand still, and seems a bit hesitant as she moves towards the front door. She doesn't seem keen on you walking her home and keeps saying, 'I must be going!'

What will it be?

(I'll be a gentleman and see her home.)

You are at the front door.

Diane can't stand still. She's virtually walking on the spot.

But she doesn't say anything.

(I'll be a gentleman and see her home.)

You get ready to leave the house. You open the front door.

DIANE: There's no need for you –

YOU: Oh, but I must see you home. I won't be a minute.

While you get ready, she is walking up and down in the little porch. Her hands are clenched at her sides. She is biting her lip.

Suddenly she does a little curtsey and hurriedly crosses one leg over the other.

She turns to you, red with embarrassment, and gasps, 'I must use your toilet.' Then she hurries back into the house.

(You wait for her.)

Diane has rushed to the loo. She is there some time.

Eventually she returns looking flushed and ill at ease. She tries to smile at you and only half-heartedly returns your kiss.

As you embrace, you sense something different. You've got used to her perfume this evening, but is there something else, a slight aroma of urine?

Has she wet herself?

You kiss her goodnight and then go up into the bathroom for a pee. This being a house occupied by two blokes, the bathroom is far from immaculate.

But on the floor in front of the toilet there is a hint of shininess – as if someone has been mopping up a little puddle.
