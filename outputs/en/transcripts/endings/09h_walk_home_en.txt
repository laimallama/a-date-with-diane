Outdoor Endings – Diane Pees Behind the Skip

Diane is only answering in monosyllables. She is hurrying along, slightly bent forward. She is no longer holding your hand.

She has one hand deep in her dress pocket.

Suddenly she stops in her tracks and shoves a hand to her crotch. She gasps, 'Oh God!' Then hurries on ahead of you.

She is about ten yards ahead of you. Suddenly she stops again, looks frantically left and right.

She is standing close to a skip, loaded with old bricks. With a couple of steps she has gone behind it.

(You hurry to catch up.)

Within a few steps you have caught her up. But then you stop, four or five yards behind her.

Diane is standing there, behind the skip, with her back to you. But there is no mistaking what has happened.

The poor girl couldn't hold on any longer.

(You watch in wonder.)

The darkness is lit by a street light, and in the half light you can see a puddle beginning to form around her feet, and hear the splatter of pee on the pavement.

She is slightly bent forward, legs a little apart, one hand on her thigh, the other apparently under her dress at the front. You presume she is holding her knickers aside, as a stream of pee falls between her legs, splashing onto the pavement.

And her shoes seem to be glistening with wetness.

You watch silently, until the pee dwindles to a trickle, and you hear what sounds like a little sob.

(What are you going to do?)

Eventually Diane steps a couple of feet away from the puddle.

She lets her dress fall back into position.

She moves slowly. She doesn't say anything, but seems to be trying to stifle a sob. She turns towards home but doesn't look towards you.

She bends down to wipe her legs.

What do you do?

(Offer her a tissue?)

She turns towards home, but doesn't look at you. You go quietly up to her and put an arm round her shoulders.

She is desperately embarrassed and on the verge of tears.

DIANE: (almost inaudibly) I'm sorry. I'm sorry. I just couldn't wait.

YOU: (softly) It's all right. It's nothing to be ashamed of.

DIANE: I'm so embarrassed.

You hold her closer, towards you, her head on your shoulder. You stroke her hair.

You kiss her forehead. When she draws away, you gently ask if she wants a tissue. She nods.

(You give her a tissue.)

Diane takes the tissue with a scarcely audible 'thank you' and starts wiping her legs.

DIANE: Actually – d'you mind if I catch you up?

You can take a hint so you say you'll walk on a bit, which you do – but once in the darkness you do try to glance back. Diane is still wiping down her legs.

She looks left and right, steps further into the shadows, but you can still just about make out what she is doing.

She reaches under her dress, then slips her knickers down.

She looks around and then tosses them into the skip. You make a mental note of whereabouts they are.

(A couple of minutes later she rejoins you.)

Diane rejoins you. She seems strangely vulnerable.

You hold her hand and squeeze it. She responds. But she is still not speaking and seems on the verge of tears.

You put an arm round her and hold her close to you.

DIANE: (very quietly) I feel so embarrassed. But thank you for being so nice about it.

You give her a hug. What are you going to say next?

(Think about it.)

You are only a few yards away from Diane's house. What are you going to do next?

Think carefully about it. You are almost there. First Prize is within your grasp.

(Tell her you'll pretend you didn't see anything.)

YOU: Don't worry. I didn't really see anything. Nobody will know.

But she doesn't believe you. And after a quick kiss she is gone.

But at least she has agreed to see you again next week.

As you walk back home, you go past the skip. The ground is quite dry but next to the skip a puddle glistens in the half light.

And as you look at it, you feel a wave of sympathy for Diane. The poor girl must have been in misery, trying to summon up the courage to pop behind a hedge.

You walk on and then you pause. You turn back.

(You look at the skip.)

You stop and look around. You reach into the skip.

Your hand encounters a pair of white knickers.

The gusset is still soaking wet. You take them home as a souvenir.

(You came so close to winning, but at least –)

YOU HAVE WON A CONSOLATION PRIZE!
