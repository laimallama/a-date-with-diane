At the Theatre – Diane Needs the Loo During the Play

The play continues. This is the bit you really enjoyed!

The girl who had been sulking in her car returns, because she needs the loo, but she can't get into the clubhouse without passing the others.

As a result, she stands at the side of the stage, looking across, hopping from foot to foot.

You imagine Diane like that, in an hour or two's time. But then dismiss the thought as too much imagination.

You look at Diane beside you.

Diane is dying for the loo! She's leaning forward, her hands on her knees, shifting her bottom from side to side. You notice her looking around, biting her lip.

You look back at the stage.

(The actress really knows how to play desperation.)

The play continues. The girl still hasn't got into the clubhouse and is getting more and more desperate.

She keeps hiding from view and then emerging to see if the coast is clear, squirming and fidgeting about.

The audience find it hilarious.

Somehow Diane doesn't seem to be finding it so funny.

(You're enjoying the play.)

The play continues. The girl is so desperate that she is running on the spot, holding her crotch.

Finally she makes a dash for it, past the other characters, into the clubhouse.

You laugh with the rest of the audience. You squeeze Diane's hand and look at her.

She looks at you with a forced smile.

You lost 5 intimacy points.

You really enjoyed that scene.

(The play continues.)

There is another slight pause for a scene change.

Diane has gone very quiet, and is biting her lip.

Diane turns to you and whispers: 'Do you know how long this interval will be?'

YOU: I think it's just a scene change. Probably only a minute.

She has picked her bag up and has it on her lap. She pretends to be getting a hankie out but is looking around.

DIANE: I wonder if there's time for –

(The lights dim.)

The lights have dimmed but there seems to be a slight pause.

You offer Diane the bottle of water.

She shakes her head.

(The play continues.)

The play is coming to a conclusion.

The characters get more and more confused.

They come and they go.

Diane seems rather distracted. She is leaning a bit forward, her handbag on her lap.

Diane can't sit still. She's dying for a pee during the play. You gained 5 shyness points.

(The play has almost ended.)

The play comes to an end.

The curtain closes.

And the cast take a bow.

You both applaud along with the audience.

YOU: I really enjoyed it.

She says, 'So did I. Thank you for taking me to it.'

She gives your cheek a little kiss.

(You stand up to leave the theatre.)

The play has ended. It is 9.30 and the last bus home leaves at 11.15.

What are you going to do next? It seems you are going to meet Diane's friend Molly, the one in the play.

And then at some stage you'll need a walk along the riverside to get to the Pavilion for your bus home – and hopefully have some time alone with Diane on the way.

Take a moment to think of it.

(In the meantime everyone is filing out of the auditorium.)

So which will it be?

(Meet Molly at the stage door and maybe have a drink somewhere else?)

You're going to meet Diane's friend Molly outside at the stage door.

The audience are filing out of the auditorium. There is quite a crush and it is taking some time to reach the foyer.

However, one of the fire doors at the side has also been opened, so many people are exiting that way. It leads straight into the street.

YOU: We can go this way. The stage door will be just round the corner.

Diane is taken by surprise that you are going this way. She trails behind. She had been expecting to go out into the foyer. She looks a bit put out but says nothing.

You are out in the street. You work your way past the theatregoers –

(– and head towards the stage door.)

The stage door is just to the right, at the back of the theatre. It's a gloomy little door, but one or two people are coming out, cheerfully saying goodnight to the doorman.

It is early summer and darkness is moving in.

The cool evening air begins to work its magic on Diane's bladder.

(You wait for Molly.)

You're both waiting at the stage door.

Diane is scarcely listening. She keeps pacing in front of the posters, unable to stand still. She had probably been hoping to use the Ladies in the foyer.

(You wait for Molly.)

Diane is fidgeting about, shifting from foot to foot. She stands on one leg, then moves around, all the while trying to chat. She can't stand still. Suddenly she stops in mid-sentence, and blushes bright red.

DIANE: I'm sorry. I need to go to the toilet. I thought we'd be going into the foyer and – You'll recognise Molly. Just wait here and I'll be back in a couple of minutes.

She hurries away.

A minute or so later Molly appears, with a male friend. You go up to her and introduce yourself, and say Diane has just dashed to the loo. Molly laughs.

MOLLY: That sounds like Diane.

The three of you chat and you say how much you enjoyed the play. After two or three minutes Diane returns, grinning ruefully. She and Molly hug and kiss.

DIANE: Sorry about that.

You hear her whisper to Molly.

DIANE: I was desperate!

She's embarrassed at having had to dash to the loo. You gained 2 shyness points.
